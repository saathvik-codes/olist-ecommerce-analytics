reviewed orders; 6.66% of them arrived after the promised date.

## Reading it

Late orders are rated about 2.02 points lower on a 5-point scale, and are
6.7x as likely to receive a 1-2 star review. The rank-biserial correlation of
0.638 is a large effect by the usual conventions. With n this large the p-value carries
no information beyond "not zero"; the effect sizes are the result.

Across all orders the monotonic link between delivery days and score is weaker
(Spearman rho -0.2349): most orders arrive in a normal window where a day more or less
barely matters. The damage is concentrated in the orders that break the promise.

## Assumption checks

* Scale: ordinal and skewed (median 5 on time), so a rank test and bootstrap CIs were used
  instead of a t-test.
* Independence: repeating the test on each customer's first order only (n = 92,735)
  gives AUC 0.8194, mean difference 2.0227
  (CI 1.982 to 2.062) and risk ratio 6.74 - effectively unchanged.
* Causality: observational. Late orders are also slower in absolute terms and skew towards
  remote states; test 2 separates those factors.
* Missing reviews: orders without a review are excluded; if unhappy customers review more
  (or less) often, the absolute rates shift, but both groups are affected.
